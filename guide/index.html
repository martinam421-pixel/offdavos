Davos, explained

The mountain village nobody had time to understand before arriving.

Zürich HB→Landquart→Davos

Getting here

Everyone is getting off. Don't panic.

You're in Landquart, not Davos. From Zürich HB, every route to Davos changes here onto the red Rhaetian Railway (RhB). It is not a downgrade. It is the point.

The train turned red? Your ticket to Davos still covers it. One journey, one ticket.

Buy it before you board. No gates, no buying on the train. Inspectors check, and fine on the spot.

Trains · read this before you board

Your train might split in half.

Wrong half: wrong valley. Your meeting starts without you.

Some RhB trains are two trains coupled together. At Klosters Platz they split: one half goes to Davos, the other to St. Moritz. Nobody comes to fetch you. You notice when the mountains look unfamiliar and your badge pick-up closes.

And Davos has two stations. Except during WEF: then it has three. Dorf, Platz, and the temporary Kongress / Eisstadion stop, where not every part of the train opens its doors. Check the display in your carriage.

Luggage

Your room isn't ready. Your suitcase has two options.

Davos Platz: staffed counter 07:20–18:45 with luggage storage, plus lockers. Only four are large. Davos Dorf: lockers only, nobody there.

Big suitcase? Ask your hotel or host to hold it.

Getting around

Taxi? Look at traffic. Then at your feet.

Roads close at short notice, motorcades happen, security zones move. For short distances, walking is usually faster. Wear soles with grip: the pavement is ice.

Walking

Your shortcut doesn't exist this week.

The path through the Kurpark, between the Congress Centre and the ice stadium, was closed during WEF in 2024, 2025 and 2026. In 2026 the lakeside path at the Davosersee was shut from 5 to 29 January. Follow the signed detours. Good luck.

Buses

Your bus stop may have moved.

During WEF, big buses stop at the edges of town and small WEF buses run the centre, roughly every 15 minutes, with no fixed timetable. Stops move days before WEF starts.

2026: all local VBD buses free for everyone. 2027: not announced yet.

Evening

The train that brought you may not take you back.

The Dorf–Kongress–Platz train shuttle stops in the evening. In 2025 and 2026, a bus took over from about 20:00.

Dorf ↔ Platz →

Night

The last bus isn't the last bus.

Davos has an on-demand night bus: invia PickMeUp. In town it runs 00:00–06:00 every winter night. Book in the app at least 30 minutes ahead. Pay by card in the app.

Your guest card is NOT valid on it. No cash.

Money

The most expensive CHF 5.90 in Davos.

You paid for free trains. Use them.

CHF 5.90 a night gets you the guest card: free trains from Davos Laret to Davos Glaris and free local buses. Ask your hotel or host for it, or get it yourself at Talstrasse 41, Davos Platz.

Rented out your flat for WEF? Congratulations. You're a landlord now.

By car

Your parking ticket doesn't buy you the night.

1 November to 30 April: no parking on public spaces 03:00–07:00, for snow clearing. Police issued 359 parking fines during WEF 2026.

Driving in? Security checks on the access roads can mean waiting time. Leave a buffer.

Electric car? The chargers at Seeparkplatz and Kongressparkplatz exist. You just can't drive to them during WEF.

Ski

Two mountains. Zero lifts.

Rinerhorn and Pischa close during WEF week. In 2026 they were shut 19–23 January, and the official operating times for this winter say the same. Parsenn, Jakobshorn and Madrisa stay open.

Closed for WEF

The swimming pool is inside the security zone.

The eau-là-là pool was closed during WEF in 2025 and 2026, because it sits inside the security zone. The library stayed open. Bring a book, not your swimsuit.

Guide · The funny part

Survive CH

Switzerland in twelve rules. Useful, and a little bit mean.

Time

08:21 means 08:21.

Swiss trains leave on the minute. Your meeting may run late. The train will not wait for it.

Tickets

No gates. Not an invitation.

Buy before you board: SBB app or the machine on the platform. Not on the train. Inspectors are real, fines are real, and "I didn't know" is not a ticket.

Plugs

Your charger may not fit.

Swiss sockets are Type J. Slim two-pin EU plugs fit. Chunky round German ones don't. UK and US plugs need an adapter. Everyone else forgot theirs.

Water

Tap water is free.

And excellent. Drink it. At 1,560 metres, the second glass of wine counts like the third.

Sunday

Sunday is closed.

Most shops shut on Sundays. Station shops are usually the exception. Plan toothpaste emergencies for Saturday.

Language

Say "Grüezi".

That's hello. Everyone speaks English. The local word gets you further.

Money

CHF. Not EUR.

Cards work almost everywhere. Euros sometimes, change comes back in francs at their rate. Terminal offers your home currency? Say no. Pay in CHF.

Tipping

Americans, please remain calm.

This is not a 25% tip emergency. Service is included. Rounding up is normal.

Phone

Switzerland is not in the EU.

Your EU roaming plan may not cover it. Check before you land, or get an eSIM. Your phone bill can be the most expensive panel you attend.

Shoes

The Shoe Risk Scale.

1 Winter boots with grip. 5 Sneakers: your socks will know by 10:00. 7 Smooth leather soles: chosen for the panel, not the pavement. 10 Heels, at night, downhill, after the reception. Louboutins: stay at home. The fix: slip-on spikes over the shoes you already have.

Beer

Yes, you can drink a beer on the train.

Switzerland trusts you with alcohol. Not with train tickets. Make it a local one: Calanda, brewed in Chur, down the line. Monsteiner, brewed in Monstein, which is technically Davos. Or Feldschlösschen, the one on every Swiss tap.

Night

Your after-party is your neighbours' business.

Apartments have house rules, and evening quiet hours are normal. Davos is a village with a conference, not the other way round.

Too cold in Davos?

Drinking makes you feel warm. It doesn't make you warm.

Alcohol opens the blood vessels in your skin. You feel cosy, your body loses heat faster. At −10 °C that's not a vibe, that's physics.

The exit strategy

Take the night bus. Or a taxi. Or a friend.
