SURVIVE
SWITZERLAND.

Things Switzerland apparently assumed you already knew.

Tickets · the 10-second version

Tickets.

Ticket gates?

None. That's not an invitation.

When do I buy?

Before you board. Not on the train.

Where?

SBB app or the machine on the platform.

"I didn't know"?

Is not a ticket. Inspectors are real, fines are real.

Money

Money.

Currency?

Swiss francs. CHF.

Card?

Accepted almost everywhere.

Euros?

Sometimes. Change comes back in francs, at their rate.

TWINT?

The Swiss payment app. You'll see the logo everywhere.

The coffee is not a typo. That's just the price.

Tipping

Americans, please remain calm.

This is not a 25% tip emergency.

Service is included. Rounding up is normal.

Plugs

Plugs.

Socket type?

Type J. Three small holes in a hexagon.

Slim EU two-pin?

Fits.

Chunky round German plug?

Doesn't fit.

UK or US plug?

Bring an adapter. Everyone else forgot theirs.

Roaming

Roaming.

EU?

Switzerland is not in it.

So my EU plan works?

Not automatically. Check your roaming rates before you land.

Easy fix?

An eSIM or a Swiss prepaid SIM.

Your phone bill can be the most expensive panel you attend.

Supermarkets

Migros.

What is it?

One of the two big Swiss supermarkets. The orange M.

Wine for the apartment?

Not here. Migros traditionally doesn't sell alcohol.

Supermarkets

Coop.

What is it?

The other big one. Sells wine and beer.

In Davos?

Yes, including Coop Davos Platz.

Migros or Coop?

Ask a Swiss person. Then get comfortable.

Rules nobody thought to mention

Rules nobody thought to mention.

Sunday is closed.

Most shops shut on Sundays. Station shops are usually the exception. Plan toothpaste emergencies for Saturday.

08:21 means 08:21.

Trains leave on the minute. Your meeting may run late. The train will not wait for it.

Tap water is free and excellent.

Drink it. At 1,560 m, the second glass of wine counts like the third.

Quiet at night.

Apartments have house rules, and quiet hours in the evening are normal. Your after-party is your neighbours' business too.

Say "Grüezi".

That's hello. Everyone speaks English, but the local word gets you further.
