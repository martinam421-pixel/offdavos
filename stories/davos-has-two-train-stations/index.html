Trains

DAVOS HAS TWO TRAIN STATIONS. EXCEPT DURING WEF.

Then it has three.

Under normal circumstances, arriving in Davos is relatively straightforward.

Davos Dorf

Parsenn side.

Davos Kongress / Eisstadion

Temporary WEF stop for the Congress Centre / ice stadium area.

Davos Platz

Jakobshorn side.

During WEF, Rhätische Bahn adds a temporary stop between them: Davos Kongress / Eisstadion.

Yes. Switzerland builds you another train station for the week.

Everyone is getting off. Don't panic.

You're in Landquart.

No, you're not in Davos yet.

Getting here

Zürich → Landquart → RhB → Davos

Change at Landquart onto the red narrow-gauge Rhätische Bahn.

It is not a downgrade.

IT IS THE POINT.

The train turned red. Do I need another ticket?

Not if your valid through-ticket already covers Davos. Changing from SBB to RhB does not automatically mean buying another ticket.

Tickets

Switzerland has no ticket gates.

This is not an invitation. Have a valid ticket before departure.

Inspectors are real. Fines are real. And “I didn't know” is not a ticket.

You're on the right train. Are you in the right half?

RhB Capricorn trains can run as several complete trainsets coupled together.

At Klosters Platz, a coupled service can split. One section can continue towards Davos while another heads towards the Engadin / St. Moritz.

RhB calls this Flügeln.

Which sounds considerably more romantic than:

Klosters

You may be sitting in the wrong half of the train.

Check the destination displays and announcements. RhB's own example has Davos passengers in the rear section when the front continues towards St. Moritz.

Your train stops here. Your part of the train might not.

During WEF, the temporary Kongress/Eisstadion stop creates one more detail worth checking.

Kongress / Eisstadion

Getting off at Kongress? Check which section stops there.

Not every part of a long train may open its doors at the temporary stop. Watch the displays and listen to the announcements. We add the confirmed section for WEF 2027 as soon as RhB publishes it.
