Story 03 · People

THE PEOPLE WHO ACTUALLY KEEP DAVOS RUNNING DURING WEF

No, not the CEOs.

While some people come to Davos to discuss the future of work, others are working very long days to make the whole thing possible.

Hotels. Bars. Kitchens. Security. Drivers. Cleaners. Technicians. Event crews.

The people who make sure the people improving the state of the world have fresh towels.

The shifts

Before the first delegate has breakfast, someone has already cleaned the lobby, checked the heating, cleared the snow and set up the room where the future of work will be discussed.

After the last delegate goes to bed, someone is still resetting the room, restocking the bar, clearing the snow again and preparing for the same thing tomorrow.

During WEF, a normal shift can become a very long shift. A very long shift can become two shifts. The town doesn't close. It just changes pace.

The guests

Some guests are polite. Some are demanding. Some are both, simultaneously, at high speed.

A VIP request at 2am is still a request. A last-minute change for 300 people is still a change. A dietary requirement invented three days ago is still a dietary requirement.

The people handling these requests are professionals. They solve problems you didn't know existed, in a language you may not speak, at a speed you probably can't match.

The town

Davos during WEF is not just the Congress Centre. It's the hotels, the restaurants, the bars, the shuttle buses, the security checkpoints, the pop-up venues and the temporary infrastructure that appears for one week and disappears the next.

All of this is run by people. Local people. Seasonal workers. People who came for the winter and stayed for the chaos.

They are the reason any of it works.

The ask

If you're in Davos during WEF, and someone is serving you, driving you, cleaning your room, cooking your food, checking your badge, directing your taxi, carrying your bags or clearing your table —

They are not the help.

They are the people keeping the whole thing running.

Tip them. Thank them. Be patient.

They are working harder than you.
