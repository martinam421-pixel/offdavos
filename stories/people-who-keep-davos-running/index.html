People / Local reality

Who actually runs Davos during WEF?

No, not the CEOs.

For one week in January, Davos hosts the people who run the world.

And the people who run Davos.

Hotels. Bars. Kitchens. Security. Drivers. Cleaners. Technicians. Event crews.

The people who make sure the people improving the state of the world have fresh towels.

Before anyone else is awake

Before the first delegate has breakfast, the lobby is clean, the heating works, the snow is gone and the room where the future of work will be discussed is ready.

After the last delegate goes to bed, the room is reset, the bar is restocked and the snow is back. So someone clears it again.

During WEF, a normal shift becomes a long shift. A long shift becomes two.

The town doesn't close.
It just changes pace.

The real experts in the room

Some guests are polite.
Some are demanding.
Some are both, simultaneously, at high speed.

A VIP request at 2am.
A last-minute change for 300 people.
A dietary requirement invented three days ago.

Handled. Usually before the guest has finished explaining it.

These are professionals. They solve problems you didn't know existed, in a language you may not speak, at a speed you probably can't match.

They know which road is closed before you've opened your map. Which hotel entrance actually works. Where the queue moves and where it doesn't.

Delegates come for a week. They do this every year.

The town

Davos during WEF is not just the Congress Centre.

It's the hotels, the restaurants, the bars, the shuttle buses, the checkpoints, the pop-up venues and the temporary infrastructure that appears for one week and disappears the next.

All of it is run by people.
Locals.
Seasonal workers.
People who came for the winter and stayed for the chaos.

They are the reason any of it works.

If you're visiting

You're used to people doing things for you. Fine.
This week, notice them.

Someone will serve you, drive you, clean your room, cook your food, check your bag, direct your taxi, carry your luggage and clear your table.

They are not the help.
They are not your staff.
They are the reason your week works.

Your title doesn't work here. Their name does. Learn it.

Nobody at the bar has read your keynote.
Shouting doesn't clear the snow faster.
A 2am request is fine. A 2am request with an attitude is a choice.

Say thank you. Round up. Wait your turn.

They are working harder than you.
And they remember how you behaved.
