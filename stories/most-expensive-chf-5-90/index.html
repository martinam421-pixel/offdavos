THE MOST EXPENSIVE CHF 5.90 IN DAVOS

Paying CHF 8,000 for an apartment does not make the CHF 5.90 disappear.

During WEF, you can spend thousands of francs on an apartment in Davos for a few nights.

There will be security convoys outside. Someone nearby will be discussing the future of capitalism.

And Switzerland would still like to talk to you about CHF 5.90.

Per person. Per night.

That is the current Davos guest tax for guests in hotels, holiday apartments and camping accommodation.

The interesting part is not really the CHF 5.90.

It is the machinery behind it.

During WEF, apartments that may spend most of the year as ordinary homes suddenly become some of the most expensive temporary accommodation in Switzerland. Guests arrive through hotels, private hosts, agencies and short-term rental platforms. But the small municipal guest tax does not disappear just because the rent has acquired another zero.

Even an apartment rented only during WEF can fall under the guest-tax and registration rules.

So who registers the guest? Who collects the tax? Who issues the card? And what happens when the person paying several thousand francs for a bed has never heard of any of this?

That is where CHF 5.90 becomes more interesting than it looks.

For paying guests in qualifying accommodation, proper registration can lead to the Davos Klosters Premium Card. The card can include local public transport within the applicable zones, plus other destination benefits.

In other words: the smallest line item in your WEF accommodation may be the one that stops you buying another local train ticket.

Hotels generally handle the process. With a paid holiday apartment, the host or accommodation provider needs to handle the registration and guest tax. If an agency is involved, ask who is responsible for the registration and card rather than assuming somebody else has done it.

For free/private stays, a different Private Card category applies. Owners and long-term tenants have different rules again.

The practical question for most visitors is therefore very simple:

Guest card

Where's my guest card?

If you are paying for accommodation and have not received the relevant guest-card information, start with the hotel, host, provider or agency that arranged the accommodation.

The card matters most when Davos is at its least normal.

During WEF, traffic restrictions, security zones and sheer volume can make short local journeys unexpectedly annoying. If your guest card already covers the local bus or RhB journey you need, that is one less thing to solve at a ticket machine.

For a Davos-based qualifying card, current local transport coverage includes the applicable Davos zones and RhB travel in the Davos area. Exact validity depends on the card and accommodation location, so check the current official coverage rather than assuming 'free local transport' means unlimited travel across Graubünden.

And this leads neatly to another Swiss detail.

Tickets

Switzerland expects you to have a valid ticket before you travel.

There are no ticket gates at most Swiss stations. This is not an invitation.

If your guest card covers the journey, good. If it does not, have a valid ticket before departure.

Which becomes particularly relevant during WEF, because Davos has another small transport surprise.

Davos normally has two train stations.

Except during WEF.

Then it has three.
