Money / Local reality

The most expensive CHF 5.90 in Davos.

Switzerland would still like its CHF 5.90.

During WEF, you can spend thousands of francs on an apartment in Davos for a few nights. There will be security convoys outside. Someone nearby will be discussing the future of capitalism.

And Switzerland would still like to talk to you about CHF 5.90.

Per person. Per night.

That is the current Davos guest tax for hotels and holiday apartments.

The CHF 5.90 itself isn't the interesting part. The machinery behind it is.

During WEF, apartments that spend most of the year as ordinary homes suddenly become some of the most expensive temporary accommodation in Switzerland. Guests arrive through hotels, private hosts, agencies and short-term rental platforms. But the small municipal guest tax does not disappear just because the rent has acquired another zero.

The part almost nobody knows

Rent out your apartment for WEF week — even once, even if you're only the long-term tenant — and Davos considers you a landlord.

You register your guests. You're jointly liable for their guest tax. And you pay a tourism levy of CHF 65 per bed, per year.

It doesn't matter whether you own the place, rent it long-term or live in Davos all year. Renting it out during WEF follows the same rules.

So who registers the guest? Who collects the tax? Who issues the card? And what happens when the person paying several thousand francs for a bed has never heard of any of this?

That is where CHF 5.90 becomes more interesting than it looks.

What you get for it

Pay the guest tax and you get a Davos Klosters guest card. For paying guests, that card can include local public transport within the applicable zones, plus other destination benefits.

In other words: the smallest line item in your WEF accommodation may be the one that stops you buying another local train ticket.

Hotels generally handle the process. With a paid apartment, the host can register you in advance. If they haven't, you can do it yourself: walk into the information office, pay the CHF 5.90, get the card.

If an agency is involved, ask who is handling your registration rather than assuming somebody else has done it.

Guest card

Where's my guest card?

Ask whoever provided your accommodation. Or go straight to the information office: Talstrasse 41, Davos Platz.

The card matters most when Davos is at its least normal. During WEF, traffic restrictions, security zones and sheer volume can make short local journeys unexpectedly annoying. If your guest card already covers the local bus or RhB journey you need, that is one less thing to solve at a ticket machine.

Exact validity depends on the card and where you are staying, so check the current official coverage rather than assuming “free local transport” means unlimited travel across Graubünden.

And this leads neatly to another Swiss detail.

Tickets

Switzerland expects you to have a valid ticket before you travel.

There are no ticket gates at most Swiss stations. This is not an invitation.

If your guest card covers the journey, good. If it does not, have a valid ticket before departure.

Which becomes particularly relevant during WEF, because Davos has another small transport surprise.

Davos normally has two train stations. Except during WEF. Then it has three.

How it works

Guest taxCHF 5.90 per person per night (hotels, holiday apartments)
Who registersThe host in advance, or the guest themselves at the information office
Where you payInformation office: you pay, you get the guest card
Host's liabilityJointly liable for the guests' tax
Host's levy (TFA)CHF 65 per rented bed, per year, after registering as a landlord
WEF-only rentals?Same rules — for owners, long-term tenants and locals alike
Misuse / evasionPunishable; evaded tax and levy must be paid back
